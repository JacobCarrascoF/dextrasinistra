Right-hand guitar exercises

Your picking hand sets the rhythm, the attack and how clean everything sounds. Train it like the fretting hand: short exercises, a metronome, and a tempo that only goes up when the take is clean.

Why train the picking hand on its own

Many mistakes that seem to come from the fretting hand start in the picking hand: a pick stroke that lands late, a string crossing that rushes, or one note louder than the rest.

Play the right hand alone, on open strings, and the left hand can no longer hide those mistakes. You hear them straight away.

The techniques to work on

  • Alternate picking: down and up, always alternating, even when you change strings.
  • String crossing: moving between strings without losing the pulse. This is where lack of control shows most.
  • Economy and sweep: using the pick's motion when you change strings in the same direction.
  • Hybrid picking: pick plus middle and ring fingers, for string skips and open voicings.
  • Fingerstyle (p i m a): thumb, index, middle and ring, for arpeggios and fingerpicking.
  • Strumming and rhythm: the arm moving like a steady pendulum, even when you don't hit every string.

One exercise to start today

String crossing with alternate picking, open strings only. Set the metronome to 60 BPM and play eighth notes: four on the A string, four on the D string, without stopping.

D|---------0-0-0-0-|
A|-0-0-0-0---------|
   ↓ ↑ ↓ ↑ ↓ ↑ ↓ ↑

↓ downstroke · ↑ upstroke. Keep alternating when you change strings.

When it comes out clean four times in a row, add 4 BPM. If your arm or hand feels tense, drop 10% and build back up another day.

And with fingers

The same work for fingerstyle: an arpeggio on open strings with p i m a, each finger on its own string.

e|-------0-------0-|
B|-----0-------0---|
G|---0-------0-----|
E|-0-------0-------|
   p i m a p i m a

p thumb · i index · m middle · a ring.

How to raise the tempo without tension

  1. Start where it's clean. The whole exercise, complete, sounding good and in time.
  2. Clean: +4 BPM next time. Small, frequent steps beat big jumps.
  3. Controlled: keep the tempo. You're nearly there: repeat it another day.
  4. Tense: drop 10%. Tension gets learned just like the movement, so don't practise it.

Frequently asked questions

How long should I practise the right hand each day?

10 to 15 minutes a day shows results within a few weeks, as long as it's regular and with a metronome. A little every day beats a lot on one day.

Alternate or economy picking?

Both. Alternate picking gives a very even pulse; economy saves motion when changing strings. It's worth mastering alternate picking first.

Does it work for classical or acoustic guitar?

Yes. The fingerstyle exercises (p i m a) work on any guitar, and the pick exercises suit electric and acoustic.

Do I need to read standard notation?

No. Every exercise comes in tab.
