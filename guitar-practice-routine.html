A guitar practice routine you'll actually keep

Practising a lot isn't the same as practising well. A good routine decides what to play, for how long and at what tempo, so all you have to do is pick up the guitar.

Four rules of a good routine

  1. Little and often. Five days of 25 minutes beat one three-hour Sunday.
  2. Both hands, every day. One exercise for the picking hand, one for the fretting hand and a block with both together.
  3. Always with a metronome. It's the only way to know you're improving.
  4. Note how it went. Tense, controlled or clean: that tells you where to start tomorrow.

Three routines for the time you have

25 minutes · beginner5 min warm-up · 8 min picking hand · 8 min fretting hand · 4 min both hands.
40 minutes · intermediate5 min warm-up · 12 min picking hand · 12 min fretting hand · 11 min both hands or repertoire.
60 minutes · advanced10 min warm-up · 15 min picking hand · 15 min fretting hand · 20 min both hands: scales, arpeggios, voicings.

Five days a week, two days off. On busy days, do a short 10 or 20-minute version: keeping the streak matters more than the length.

When to change exercises

An exercise stays in your routine for about three weeks: two raising the tempo little by little and one playing it comfortably, below your record, to lock it in. If it ends up clean at the target tempo, a new one comes in; if not, it stays one more week.

How to raise the tempo

  • Clean: next time, 4 BPM faster.
  • Controlled: same tempo.
  • Tense: drop 10%.

Frequently asked questions

How long should I practise guitar each day?

Between 20 and 60 minutes, five days a week, is a solid base. What matters is consistency and practising with a metronome.

Do I need to warm up before playing?

Yes. Five minutes of gentle exercises prepare your hand and prevent tension, especially before playing fast.

What if I skip a day?

Nothing serious. Pick the plan up where you left it; the week counts, not a single day.

Does it work for any style?

Yes. Two-hand technique is the base of rock, jazz, pop, flamenco and classical.
